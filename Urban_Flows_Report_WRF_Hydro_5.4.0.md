# Technical Report: Urban Water Flow Simulation in WRF-Hydro 5.4.0

## 1. Introduction
This report provides a detailed technical analysis of how urban water flows are simulated in the WRF-Hydro 5.4.0 model. The simulation mechanism primarily relies on the interaction between the Noah-MP Land Surface Model (LSM) for column physics and the diffusive wave routing module for overland flow. There is no explicit sewer network model in the baseline version; instead, urban hydrological response is parameterized through modifications to infiltration, runoff generation, and surface roughness.

## 2. Runoff Generation (Noah-MP LSM)

In WRF-Hydro, the vertical water balance is handled by the Noah-MP LSM. Urban areas are identified by the `ISURBAN` parameter (typically land use category 1 in USGS, or 13 in MODIS).

### 2.1 Urban Parameters and Flags
Urban grid cells are distinguished by specific flags and parameter overrides within `module_sf_noahmplsm.F`:
-   **`parameters%urban_flag`**: A logical flag set to `.true.` for urban landuse categories.
-   **`parameters%imperv`**: The fraction of the grid cell covered by impervious surfaces. This is a critical parameter for urban hydrology.
-   **`RSURF` (Surface Resistance)**: For urban cells, `RSURF` is set to a sufficiently high value ($10^6$ s/m) to suppress soil evaporation, simulating the behavior of concrete/asphalt surfaces.
    *Code Reference:* `if (parameters%urban_flag .and. SNOWH == 0.) RSURF = 1.E6` (Line ~1950, `module_sf_noahmplsm.F`).

### 2.2 Effective Imperviousness and Runoff Logic
The model offers options for handling impervious surfaces via `OPT_IMPERV`. The relevant code block is found in `SUBROUTINE SOILWATER` (Line ~6950).

#### 2.2.1 Effective Imperviousness Calculation
The variable `imperv_eff` determines the fraction of incoming water that becomes immediate surface runoff.
-   **Case 1 (`OPT_IMPERV == 1`)**: Direct use of the input parameter.
    $$ \text{imperv\_eff} = \text{parameters\%imperv} $$
-   **Case 2 (`OPT_IMPERV == 2`)**: Adjustment based on Alley & Veenhuis (1983), accounting for connected impervious areas.
    $$ \text{imperv\_eff} = 0.0015 \times (100 \times \text{parameters\%imperv})^{1.41} $$
-   **Case 9 (`OPT_IMPERV == 9`)**: Legacy hardcoded value for specific urban types.
    $$ \text{FCR(1)} = 0.95 $$

#### 2.2.2 Runoff Partitioning
The calculated `imperv_eff` modifies the fractional saturated area (`FSAT`) and the frozen soil fraction (`FCR`). The total runoff (`RUNSRF`) is a weighted sum of runoff from the pervious and impervious portions:

$$ R_{surf} = Q_{in} \times [ (1 - F_{imp}) \times F_{sat} + F_{imp} ] $$

Where:
-   $Q_{in}$ (`QINSUR`): Net water input to the surface (Precipitation - Interception + Melt).
-   $F_{imp}$ (`FCR(1)`): The effective impervious fraction.
-   $F_{sat}$ (`FSAT`): The fraction of the permeable soil column that is saturated.

The term $Q_{in} \times F_{imp}$ represents the immediate, "pipe-like" response of urban surfaces, where infiltration is zero.

## 3. Overland Flow Routing (Diffusive Wave)

Once runoff is generated by Noah-MP (in mm/s), it is converted to a water depth/head and routed laterally across the grid by the Overland Routing module (`module_overland.F90` and `module_RT.F90`).

### 3.1 Governing Equations
The routing is governed by the **2D Diffusive Wave Equation**, a simplification of the shallow water equations.

**Continuity:**
$$ \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial q_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial q_y}{\partial y} = Q_{lat} $$

**Momentum (Manning's Approximation):**
$$ q_x = - \frac{h^{5/3}}{n \sqrt{|S_f|}} \frac{\partial (z+h)}{\partial x} $$

Where:
-   $h$: Surface water depth (`ovrt_data%control%surface_water_head_routing`).
-   $z$: Terrain elevation.
-   $n$: Manning's roughness coefficient (`roughness`).
-   $q_x, q_y$: Unit discharge in x and y directions.

### 3.2 Urban routing Parameters
The behavior of this equation in urban cells is dictated by parameter calibration in `MPTABLE.TBL` or `hydro.tbl`.

1.  **Retention Depth (`RETDEPRT`)**:
    -   Represents the depression storage that must be filled before flow occurs.
    -   **Adjustment**: In `module_RT.F90`, if `imperv_adj` is active, the retention depth is scaled down by the impervious fraction:
        $$ D_{ret} = D_{ret} \times (1 - \text{impervfrac}) $$
    -   This allows urban cells to initiate runoff much faster than rural cells.

2.  **Roughness (`OVROUGHRT`)**:
    -   Urban cells are typically assigned distinct Manning's $n$ values.
    -   Low values (e.g., 0.015) simulate smooth streets (flashy response).
    -   High values (e.g., 0.1-0.2) act as a proxy for building drag and obstacle interference, slowing down the wave propogation.

### 3.3 Numerical Scheme (`ROUTE_OVERLAND1`)
The solver uses a fully explicit, finite-difference scheme.
-   **Slope Calculation**: `sfx = (h(neighbor) - h(current)) / distance`
-   **Flux Limiting**: The flux `qqsfc` is limited by the available water depth (Courant condition check) to ensure stability.
-   **Mass Balance**: Inflows and outflows are updated iteratively (`DT_FRAC` times per LSM timestep).

## 4. Limitation of Current Approach
The current "Urban" simulation is fundamentally a **Sheet Flow** model.
-   **No Pipe Network**: All water moves on the surface.
-   **No Surcharge/Backflow**: There is no mechanism to represent limits on storm drain capacity or sewer overflow events.
-   **Topography Limited**: Flow follows the Digital Elevation Model (DEM). In reality, urban drainage often cuts across natural topography via subsurface pipes.

This structure necessitates the development of the dedicated Urban Sewer Module described in the companion design report.
